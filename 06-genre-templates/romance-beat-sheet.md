# Romance Beat Sheet

> The genre with the strictest reader contract: the central relationship **must** end in a
> believable, earned emotionally‑satisfying union (or, in some subgenres, a devastating and
> *inevitable* loss). Genre convention per Romance Writers of America craft consensus and
> Gwen Hayes's widely‑taught *Romancing the Beat*.

## The promise
Two (or more) people overcome real obstacles — external *and* internal — to earn a
relationship the reader has been rooting for. The HEA (**H**appily **E**ver **A**fter) or HFN
(**H**appy **F**or **N**ow) ending is close to non‑negotiable in the genre category itself
(a love story that ends tragically is usually shelved as "love story," not "romance").

## The beats
1. **Meet cute (or meet ugly)** — the first encounter; plant an immediate spark *or* friction.
2. **No way / resistance** — a clear reason they *shouldn't* be together (external: rivals,
   distance, duty; internal: a wound that makes intimacy dangerous for them).
3. **Attraction builds despite the resistance** — forced proximity, shared goal, or banter
   ratchets tension.
4. **The first kiss / turning point** — the relationship becomes undeniable; often mid‑book.
5. **Falling in love (the good part)** — a joyful stretch where the relationship deepens —
   this is the genre's "fun and games," the reason readers are here.
6. **The dark moment / black moment** — near the end, the central obstacle (often the
   internal wound from beat 2) resurfaces and seems to end the relationship for good.
7. **The grovel / the choice** — one or both partners must actively choose the relationship,
   proving change (not just feeling — *action*).
8. **HEA / HFN** — the union, earned specifically because of what was overcome.

## The internal‑wound rule (why romance isn't "just plot")
Each partner should have a **Lie/wound** (see
[character-arc-template.md](../05-character/character-arc-template.md)) that makes love
frightening *specifically* for them. The Black Moment should be that wound resurfacing, and
the Grovel should be them choosing vulnerability *despite* it — this is what makes the ending
feel earned instead of arbitrary.

## Variations & subversions
- **Love triangle** — a third party embodies the "wrong" choice/lie; resolved by the
  protagonist recognizing what they actually need (compare
  [character arc: Want vs. Need](../05-character/character-arc-template.md)).
- **Enemies‑to‑lovers** — beat 2 is maximized (real antagonism, not just misunderstanding);
  the "no way" must be a *genuine* conflict of goals, not a sitcom mixup.
- **Slow burn** — stretch beats 3–5 across the whole book; beat 4 moves very late.
- **Romantic subplot (not the A‑plot)** — compress all 8 beats into the [B Story](../02-story-structure/save-the-cat-15-beats.md)
  slot of a non‑romance story.

## Blank template
```text
COUPLE:                              SUBGENRE (contemporary/fantasy/historical/etc.):
PARTNER A — internal wound/Lie:      PARTNER B — internal wound/Lie:

1. MEET CUTE/UGLY —
2. NO WAY (external + internal reasons) —
3. ATTRACTION BUILDS (forced proximity / shared goal) —
4. FIRST KISS / TURNING POINT —
5. FALLING IN LOVE (the good part) —
6. BLACK MOMENT (the wound resurfaces) —
7. THE GROVEL / THE CHOICE (action, not just feeling) —
8. HEA / HFN —
```
