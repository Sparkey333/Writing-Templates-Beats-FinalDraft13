# Frame Story / Story‑Within‑a‑Story Template

> A narrator tells a story about themselves (or someone else) from a vantage point the reader
> also inhabits — the telling *itself* becomes part of the plot. The
> [Kingkiller Chronicle](../07-reference-analyses/kingkiller-chronicle-structure.md) is this
> repo's deepest worked example; see that file for a full case study.

## The promise
Dramatic irony and depth: the reader holds two timelines at once (the "now" of the telling
and the "then" of the tale), and the gap between them — what the narrator knows now that they
didn't then, why they're telling it, what they're leaving out — becomes its own source of
tension.

## Obligatory structural elements
1. **The frame present** — a "now" scene: who is telling the story, to whom, why, and under
   what constraint (a deadline, a threat, a promise, a single night).
2. **The frame's own stakes** — the frame can't just be a delivery mechanism; something must
   be at risk *in the present* too, or cutting back to it will feel like an interruption.
3. **The inner story** — the actual tale being told, which can run its own full structure
   (any framework in [`02-story-structure/`](../02-story-structure/)).
4. **The narrator's reliability** — is the teller honest, self‑serving, mistaken, or
   performing for their listener? This colors every scene of the inner story.
5. **Interludes / returns to the frame** — periodic cuts back to the present, ideally
   triggered by something in the inner story (a listener's reaction, a detail that connects
   to the frame's own stakes).
6. **Convergence** — frame and inner story eventually touch: the telling changes the present,
   or the present recontextualizes the telling (why THIS story, why NOW, why to THIS person).
7. **Close the frame** — the "now" thread resolves; ideally the act of telling has changed
   something (the teller, the listener, or the reader's understanding of both timelines).

## Craft notes
- **The "why now" question must have a real answer.** If the frame doesn't need this story
  told at this moment, the frame reads as a gimmick.
- **Let the frame's tone comment on the inner story's tone** — a present‑day narrator who's
  bitter, nostalgic, or evasive shapes how we read the "golden days" of the inner tale (this
  is Kingkiller's central technique — the innkeeper narrating his own legend).
- **Nested frames** (a story within a story within a story) raise the difficulty
  significantly — track POV and tense carefully at each layer; readers can lose track of
  which "now" they're in.

## Variations & subversions
- **The frame narrator is unreliable about the frame itself** — not just the inner tale
  (a bigger twist: the "present" wasn't what it seemed either).
- **Multiple tellers** — the inner story is assembled from conflicting accounts (a mystery
  hybrid; compare [fair-play mystery](./mystery-fair-play-template.md)).
- **The frame is simultaneous, not sequential** (Inception's nested dream levels — see
  [Inception beat sheet](../01-screenplay/beat-sheets/inception-beat-sheet.md)) — frames
  don't have to be "past vs. present"; they can be "deeper vs. shallower reality" running
  in parallel.
