# Heist Template

> A crew, a plan, and the pleasure of watching a plan meet reality — twice.
> Genre pattern drawn from the shared DNA of heist fiction/film (*Ocean's Eleven*, *The Italic
> Job*, *Inception* as a "mind‑heist" hybrid — see [Inception beat sheet](../01-screenplay/beat-sheets/inception-beat-sheet.md)).

## The promise
Competence porn: watching specialists do what they're extraordinary at, plus the dopamine hit
of a **reveal** that recontextualizes what we thought we saw.

## Obligatory beats
1. **The target** — what's being stolen/done, and why it matters (money, revenge, redemption,
   justice).
2. **Assembling the crew** — each member introduced with a *specialty* and a quick
   demonstration of it (the "getting the gang together" montage).
3. **The plan, explained** — laid out clearly enough the audience *could* follow along —
   this is heist fiction's version of [fair‑play mystery](./mystery-fair-play-template.md)
   clue‑planting: the audience needs the rules to appreciate the twist later.
4. **Complication during prep** — an obstacle forces the plan to adapt (new security, a
   double‑cross risk, a missing piece).
5. **Execution begins** — the plan in motion; tension from things nearly going wrong.
6. **The apparent failure** — it looks like it's fallen apart — caught, a betrayal, an alarm.
7. **The reveal** — recontextualize: *this was part of the plan all along* (flashback‑style
   reveal of a step we weren't shown, or weren't shown correctly).
8. **The real twist** — one layer deeper than the audience expected — often a second
   double‑cross, or the "target" was never what we were told.
9. **The clean getaway (or the cost)** — either the crew gets away clean, or the genre
   subverts into noir territory and someone pays.

## Craft technique: the "planned reveal" structure
Heist fiction routinely **shows the audience a false version of events**, then reveals the
true sequence — this only works fairly if, on rewatch/reread, the "false" version doesn't
actually lie, it just omits. (Compare the fair‑play mystery rule: omission is fair,
fabrication is not.)

## Variations & subversions
- **The heist fails for real** — no reveal saves it; a tragedy/noir hybrid (raises stakes by
  removing the genre's safety net).
- **Reverse heist** — the crew must *plant* something instead of steal it (same beat
  structure, inverted goal).
- **One-person heist** — no crew‑assembly beat; replace with establishing the protagonist's
  *range* of skills instead of a team's specialties.
- **Heist as metaphor** (*Inception*) — apply the structure to a non‑physical target
  (an idea, a memory, a secret) for a genre hybrid.
