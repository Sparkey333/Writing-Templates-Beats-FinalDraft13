# Mystery — "Fair‑Play" Clue Template

> The reader should, in principle, be able to solve the mystery before the detective explains
> it. — the "fair‑play" tradition (Ronald Knox's "Ten Commandments of Detective Fiction,"
> 1929; S.S. Van Dine's "Twenty Rules," 1928).

## The promise
A puzzle the audience can theoretically solve — and the pleasure of being *fairly* fooled:
every clue was shown, but attention was misdirected.

## Obligatory beats
1. **The crime** — usually early; a body, a theft, a locked room.
2. **The detective (and their method)** established — logic, empathy, procedure, or
   eccentricity; this *is* their characterization.
3. **The suspect pool** — introduced with means/motive/opportunity for at least 3–5 people.
4. **Clues planted in plain sight** — every clue the solution depends on must appear
   *on‑page, before the reveal.* This is the fair‑play rule; breaking it feels like cheating.
5. **Red herrings** — false leads that are *plausible*, not arbitrary — ideally clues that are
   real but point to the wrong conclusion, not fake clues invented to mislead.
6. **A false solution** — often mid‑book, the obvious suspect is accused/suspected and
   cleared, deepening the puzzle.
7. **The twist / final clue** — a piece of information recontextualizes everything (compare
   [Kishōtenketsu's](../02-story-structure/kishotenketsu.md) *ten*).
8. **The reveal** — detective explains, walking back through planted clues so the reader can
   verify (or kick themselves for missing) the logic.
9. **Resolution/justice** — consequence for the culprit; thematic closure (was justice truly
   served?).

## Fair‑play rules worth knowing (classic, still useful)
- The detective must not have information withheld from the reader (no secret clues revealed
  only at the end).
- No twins/doubles pulled from nowhere; no undisclosed supernatural solution in a "fair"
  mystery.
- The culprit should be mentioned early — not a stranger introduced in the last chapter.

## Variations & subversions
- **Inverted mystery ("howcatchem")** — reveal the culprit up front (*Columbo* structure);
  suspense shifts from *who* to *how they get caught*.
- **Unreliable narrator mystery** — the detective/narrator withholds from the *reader*, not
  just other characters (retroactively "fair" only if reread clues hold up).
- **Cozy vs. noir tone** — same skeleton, wildly different mood; see
  [noir-detective.md](./noir-detective.md) for the hard‑boiled variant.

## Blank template
```text
THE CRIME:                          VICTIM:
DETECTIVE + METHOD:
SUSPECTS (min 3-5): name | motive | opportunity | alibi
  1.
  2.
  3.
CLUES PLANTED (must appear on-page before reveal):
  1. (where planted) →
  2. (where planted) →
  3. (where planted) →
RED HERRINGS (plausible, not arbitrary):
FALSE SOLUTION (mid-story, cleared):
FINAL CLUE / TWIST (recontextualizes everything):
REVEAL — walk back through clues 1-3:
CULPRIT:                            MOTIVE:
```
