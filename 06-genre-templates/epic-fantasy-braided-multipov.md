# Epic‑Fantasy Braided Multi‑POV Template

> The George R.R. Martin / Robert Jordan school: multiple POV threads, each with its own
> local arc, braided so they eventually collide. Genre‑pattern analysis, not text from any
> specific book.

## The promise
A world big enough to need many eyes to see it, and the payoff of watching separated
characters' paths converge.

## Structural approach
Run [Seven‑Point Structure](../02-story-structure/seven-point-structure.md) or
[Three‑Act](../02-story-structure/three-act-structure.md) **once per major POV thread**, then
braid chapters so threads alternate — but design **convergence points** deliberately, not
randomly.

## Obligatory moves
1. **Each POV gets a real want and a real arc** — a POV that exists only to deliver
   exposition will read as filler. Use the
   [character dossier](../05-character/character-dossier-template.md) per thread.
2. **Geographic/political separation early** — POVs start apart for a structural reason
   (different kingdoms, different sides of a coming war, different social classes seeing
   the same world differently).
3. **Chapter‑end hooks per thread** — since you're cutting away from each POV for several
   chapters, each section needs its own cliffhanger or turn to survive the gap.
4. **Braided pacing** — a slow thread can follow a fast one to control overall tempo; use
   thread order deliberately, not just round‑robin.
5. **Convergence points (plan these first)** — 2–4 moments where separate threads meet
   physically or causally (a messenger, a battle, a shared enemy). Design backward from
   these the way [Seven‑Point Structure](../02-story-structure/seven-point-structure.md)
   designs backward from its Resolution.
6. **A shared world‑level throughline** — even while POVs diverge personally, one
   world‑stakes plot (a war, a prophecy, a collapse) ties them; see
   [worldbuilding questionnaire § Politics](../04-worldbuilding/worldbuilding-master-questionnaire.md).

## Craft notes
- **Track "the last time we saw X"** — readers forget between POV‑switches; a one‑line
  reorientation at the top of a returning thread (without over‑recapping) helps.
- **Give threads different textures** (tone, sentence rhythm, concerns) so a reader can tell
  whose chapter it is from the prose alone, not just a header.
- **Don't split evenly by default** — protagonist‑adjacent threads usually need more page
  time; minor threads can be shorter, more infrequent visits.

## Variations & subversions
- **Unreliable/contradicting POVs** — two threads witness "the same" event differently,
  creating dramatic irony only the reader holds.
- **A POV thread ends early** (character dies mid‑book) — removes a "safe" thread and raises
  stakes for all remaining ones.
- **Frame the whole braid inside one narrator** — hybridize with the
  [frame story template](./frame-story-template.md).

## Blank template
```text
WORLD-LEVEL THROUGHLINE (the shared stakes):
POV THREADS:
  Name | Location/faction | Want | Need | Convergence point(s) they hit
  ___  | ___              | ___  | ___  | ___
  ___  | ___              | ___  | ___  | ___
  ___  | ___              | ___  | ___  | ___
CONVERGENCE MAP (design backward):
  Convergence 1 — who meets, where, why, what changes:
  Convergence 2 — who meets, where, why, what changes:
  FINAL convergence (climax) — who's present, what resolves:
```
