# Redemption Arc Template

> A fall, a cost, and grace that has to be *earned* — not given. Genre‑pattern analysis,
> built on the [character arc](../05-character/character-arc-template.md) framework's
> positive‑change model applied to a character who starts genuinely villainous or complicit.

## The promise
The hardest, most satisfying kind of change: someone the story has shown doing real harm
becomes someone who deserves a second chance — and the story doesn't let them off easy for it.

## Obligatory beats
1. **Established harm** — show the character doing real damage, not just implied villainy.
   Redemption without real sin to redeem *from* feels weightless.
2. **A crack in the armor** — a moment of unexpected mercy, doubt, or humanity glimpsed
   beneath the harm — plants that change is *possible*, not yet that it's happening.
3. **A relationship that sees them as more** — someone (often a target of their harm, or an
   unlikely ally) treats them as capable of better *before they've earned it* — the
   catalytic risk that starts the arc.
4. **Refusal / backslide** — the character rejects the chance, often violently, and does more
   harm — redemption arcs that skip this beat feel too easy.
5. **The cost of the old way, revealed** — the character witnesses (or causes) a
   consequence of their path that they can no longer rationalize away.
6. **The choice** — an active, costly decision toward the better path — must be
   **action**, not just remorse (compare
   [romance's "the grovel"](./romance-beat-sheet.md) — same mechanism: feeling isn't proof,
   choice is).
7. **Atonement, not absolution** — the character *does something* to address the harm they
   caused; other characters are not obligated to forgive on schedule.
8. **The test** — old temptation/identity returns at the worst moment; they choose
   differently than beat 1, *proving* (not just stating) the change.
9. **Earned grace (or a earned, deserved cost)** — acceptance, redemption completed — or, in
   tragic variants, death/sacrifice that completes the arc without survival.

## Craft notes
- **Don't erase the harm.** The best redemption arcs let consequences persist (a relationship
  that's repaired but changed, a scar that doesn't vanish) — full erasure cheapens both the
  original harm and the redemption.
- **Redemption ≠ forgiveness on demand.** Other characters (especially victims) get to be
  slow to trust, or never fully trust again — that's realistic, not a plot hole.
- **The Toonami "redeemed rival"** (Vegeta, Piccolo) is a compressed version of this arc —
  see [toonami-shonen-powerup-arc.md § Rival & mentor archetypes](./toonami-shonen-powerup-arc.md).

## Variations & subversions
- **Failed redemption** — the character almost makes it, then chooses the old path at the
  final test — a tragedy, and a [negative arc](../05-character/character-arc-template.md).
- **Redemption via sacrifice** — the character never gets to enjoy the new life they earned;
  their final choice IS the atonement.
- **Ambiguous redemption** — the story deliberately withholds confirmation that the change
  is real/lasting (leaves the audience to judge).

## Blank template
```text
CHARACTER:                            HARM ESTABLISHED (be specific):
CRACK IN THE ARMOR (first glimpse of more) —
WHO SEES THEM AS MORE, AND WHY (before it's earned) —
REFUSAL / BACKSLIDE (more harm done) —
COST REVEALED (consequence they can't rationalize away) —
THE CHOICE (active, costly, toward better) —
ATONEMENT (what they DO, not just feel) —
THE TEST (old temptation returns) — result:
ENDING — earned grace [ ] / earned cost [ ] / ambiguous [ ]
```
