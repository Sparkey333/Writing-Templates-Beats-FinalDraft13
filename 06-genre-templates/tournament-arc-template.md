# Tournament Arc Template

> Model beats: DBZ's **World Martial Arts Tournament**, Yu Yu Hakusho's **Dark Tournament**,
> Naruto's **Chūnin Exams**. A spin‑out of the
> [Toonami/Shōnen template](./toonami-shonen-powerup-arc.md), genre‑portable beyond anime.

## The promise
A legitimized excuse to stage a run of escalating duels back‑to‑back, introduce a roster of
characters cheaply, and rank everyone's power on‑screen.

## Bracket structure
1. **Qualifying/preliminary cull** — thins the field, hides the real contenders (Chūnin
   Exams' written test + Forest of Death; the Dark Tournament's mass preliminary brawl).
2. **Single‑elimination bracket** — often teams (Dark Tournament's 16‑team format) or 1v1
   (Chūnin finals); provides a built‑in escalating‑opponent queue.
3. **Seeded reveal** — the villain team/champion sits at the far end so the hero must climb
   to reach them.

## Beat pattern of each individual fight
1. **Match‑up + stakes card** — who, and what's on the line.
2. **Feeling‑out / early dominance** — often the opponent leads.
3. **The turn** — opponent reveals a signature technique; hero is on the ropes.
4. **Flashback/exposition mid‑fight** — backstory or mechanic explained, raising investment.
5. **Comeback** — hero improvises, unlocks something, or out‑thinks the opponent.
6. **Finisher + aftermath** — named super‑move; result reshuffles the bracket; crowd/ally
   reaction sells the scale.

## Non‑anime uses
- **Sports fiction** (boxing brackets, martial arts films) — same bracket logic.
- **Competitive game fiction** (chess, card‑game tournaments, esports) — swap physical blows
  for turns/moves; the beat pattern (match‑up → dominance → turn → comeback → finisher) holds.
- **Courtroom/debate serials** — a "bracket" of hearings escalating to one final case.

## Variations & subversions
- **The hero loses a bracket match** but wins the arc's real goal (alliance, information,
  respect) — subverts "win every fight."
- **A joke/underdog entrant** wins early rounds through cleverness, not power — comic relief
  that still respects the fair‑play rules of the bracket.
- **The tournament is a trap** — the real conflict isn't the bracket, it's what the villains
  are doing *while* everyone's distracted by it (Dark Tournament model).
