# Noir / Hard‑Boiled Detective Template

> Everybody's guilty of something, including the guy telling the story.
> Genre pattern from the hard‑boiled tradition (Hammett, Chandler) and film noir convention.

## The promise
A world of pervasive moral compromise, seen through a narrator who's world‑weary but not
(quite) corrupted — and the queasy satisfaction of truth that doesn't clean anything up.

## Obligatory beats
1. **The hook** — a client, a body, or a job that seems simple and isn't.
2. **The detective's code** — established fast, usually through voice/narration: a personal
   ethic in a corrupt world (this *is* the character's arc engine — see
   [character-arc-template.md](../05-character/character-arc-template.md), often a
   **flat/steadfast arc**: the detective doesn't change, the world is revealed around them).
3. **The femme/homme fatale** — a figure of desire who is also a source of danger or
   deception; their trustworthiness is the story's central uncertainty.
4. **Escalating corruption revealed** — each answer uncovers a bigger, dirtier question;
   the mystery structure ([fair‑play template](./mystery-fair-play-template.md)) still
   applies, but the *tone* refuses catharsis.
5. **The detective is compromised** — beaten, framed, seduced, or bribed; their code is
   tested directly, not just their body.
6. **The reveal implicates power** — the truth points toward money, police, or political
   power that can bury it — noir's signature move is that **knowing isn't winning.**
7. **The ambiguous ending** — justice is partial, personal, or entirely absent; the
   detective's code survives (or doesn't), but the city doesn't get fixed.

## Voice craft (noir lives or dies on prose style)
- **First‑person hard‑boiled narration**: clipped, cynical, metaphor‑dense
  ("She had a voice like a rusty gate and a smile that cost extra").
- **Understatement over melodrama** — the bigger the violence/emotion, the flatter the
  delivery; let the gap between tone and content carry the weight.
- **Setting as moral atmosphere** — rain, smoke, neon, shadow aren't decoration; they're the
  visual argument that this world is compromised.

## Variations & subversions
- **Neo‑noir** — modern setting, same beats (corporate power instead of political bosses).
- **Sci‑fi/fantasy noir** — the detective template ported into a genre world (a magic‑using
  PI, a cyberpunk fixer) — pair with [magic system design](../04-worldbuilding/magic-system-design-template.md)
  so the noir "rules of corruption" have a fantastical analog.
- **The detective IS dirty** — subverts the code‑holder default for a
  [negative arc](../05-character/character-arc-template.md) (corruption arc).
