# 03 — Novel / Long‑Form Methods

Where [`02-story-structure/`](../02-story-structure/) gives you the *shape* of a story, this
folder gives you the *process* of building a long one — turning an idea into a scene list you
can actually draft from.

| Method | What it's for | File |
|---|---|---|
| **The Snowflake Method** ⭐ | Grow a novel from one sentence → scene list, one doubling at a time | [snowflake-method.md](./snowflake-method.md) |
| **Save the Cat! Writes a Novel** | The 15 film beats re‑scaled to novel word‑count % | [save-the-cat-writes-a-novel.md](./save-the-cat-writes-a-novel.md) |
| **9‑Block / 27‑Chapter** | A clean, symmetrical novel skeleton (3 acts × 3 blocks × 3 chapters) | [nine-block-27-chapter.md](./nine-block-27-chapter.md) |
| **Outliner vs. Discovery writer** | How much to plan — and how to blend both (Sanderson's approach) | [outliner-vs-discovery.md](./outliner-vs-discovery.md) |

**Recommended flow for a new novel:**
1. [Snowflake](./snowflake-method.md) steps 1–2 to find the spine.
2. Choose a beat shape ([Save the Cat Writes a Novel](./save-the-cat-writes-a-novel.md) or
   [9‑block](./nine-block-27-chapter.md)) and slot the disasters onto it.
3. [Worldbuild](../04-worldbuilding/) + [character dossiers](../05-character/) (Snowflake 3 & 7).
4. Snowflake 8 → scene list. Draft.

For series planning, also keep a **series bible** (persistent world + cast).
