# Save the Cat! Writes a Novel

> The 15 beats, re‑scaled from film pages to novel word‑count percentages.
> — **Jessica Brody**, *Save the Cat! Writes a Novel* (2018), adapting Blake Snyder.

Brody translated Snyder's [15‑beat sheet](../02-story-structure/save-the-cat-15-beats.md) for
prose. Same beats, expressed as **% of total word count** instead of screenplay pages, plus
the useful insight that most beats are *sections* (multiple scenes/chapters), not single moments.

## The beats at novel scale
| # | Beat | % of book | Notes for prose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening Image | 0–1% | The "before" — often chapter 1's first pages. |
| 2 | Theme Stated | ~5% | A line of dialogue that hints the lesson. |
| 3 | Set‑Up | 1–10% | Establish hero, flaws, "stasis = death," the six things that need fixing. |
| 4 | Catalyst | ~10% | The inciting incident. |
| 5 | Debate | 10–20% | Reaction; "should I?" |
| 6 | Break into Two | ~20% | Hero chooses the new world. |
| 7 | B Story | ~22% | New character(s)/relationship carrying the theme. |
| 8 | Fun and Games | 20–50% | The promise of the premise — the bulk of Act 2a. |
| 9 | Midpoint | 50% | False victory or false defeat; stakes raise. |
| 10 | Bad Guys Close In | 50–75% | Pressure mounts inside and out. |
| 11 | All Is Lost | ~75% | The low; whiff of death. |
| 12 | Dark Night of the Soul | 75–80% | Hopelessness before the answer. |
| 13 | Break into Three | ~80% | The A + B solution clicks. |
| 14 | Finale | 80–99% | Execute; five‑part finale works here too. |
| 15 | Final Image | 99–100% | The mirror "after." |

## The main difference from film
- **Beats are chapters, not pages.** "Fun and Games" might be 8 chapters. Think in scenes.
- **Interiority.** Prose can render the Debate and Dark Night internally — use it.
- **Multi‑POV.** In ensemble novels, the *protagonist's* beats set the master clock; other
  POVs sync to it.
