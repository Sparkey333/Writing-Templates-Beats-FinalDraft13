# Sanderson's World‑Building & Writing Process

Distilled from Brandon Sanderson's [free BYU lectures](../08-resources/brandon-sanderson-resources.md),
*Writing Excuses*, and his essays. His [Laws of Magic](./sanderson-laws-of-magic.md) get their
own file; this is everything else.

## Iceberg world‑building
Show ~10%; know (or imply) the rest. The reader should *feel* the submerged 90% through
confident references, not exposition dumps. Cf. Tolkien's
[illusion of depth](./tolkien-method.md).

## Hard vs. soft magic (the axis)
A continuum, not a binary — see [First Law](./sanderson-laws-of-magic.md). Most systems mix:
a **hard** core the hero uses to solve problems, wrapped in **soft** mystery that creates awe.

## MACE / MICE — the four throughlines
Every story braids some of four kinds of throughline (Orson Scott Card's **MICE**, which
Sanderson teaches as **M‑A‑C‑E**). Each opens a "promise" that must close:
- **Milieu** — a place; opens when the character enters, closes when they leave (*Gulliver's Travels*, heist‑into‑and‑out‑of).
- **Ask/Answer (Inquiry)** — a question; opens with the question, closes with the answer (mystery).
- **Character** — an identity/dissatisfaction; opens with who they are, closes when they change.
- **Event** — a disruption to the world; opens when things break, closes when a new order is set.
> **Nesting rule:** close throughlines in reverse order of opening (first opened = last
> closed), like brackets `[ { ( ) } ]`. This is why endings feel satisfying.

## Promise, Progress, Payoff
The core Sanderson pacing tool.
- **Promise:** early on, tell the reader what *kind* of story this is and what to anticipate
  (tone, a mystery, a relationship, a power, a confrontation).
- **Progress:** show measurable movement toward it (the "try/fail" cycles, the power growing).
- **Payoff:** deliver — ideally bigger and more twisted than promised.
Track each major promise across three columns; unpaid promises are what make endings flop.

## The "Sanderlanche"
Sanderson's signature: a cascading, accelerating climax where **multiple plotlines converge
and pay off in quick succession.** It works because the payoffs were all *promised and
progressed* earlier (see above) and the magic obeys the [First Law](./sanderson-laws-of-magic.md)
(so clever uses feel earned). Plan convergence: list every open promise and choreograph them
to resolve within the final stretch.

## Process: outline plot, discover character
See [outliner‑vs‑discovery](../03-novel-methods/outliner-vs-discovery.md). Sanderson outlines
the plot skeleton and the ending (so the Sanderlanche can be engineered), but discovery‑writes
character voice within it.

## Character = f(competence, proactivity, sympathy/likability)
Sanderson's "sliders" for making a character compelling: adjust how **competent**, how
**proactive**, and how **likable/sympathetic** they are. Raising any one raises reader
investment; heroes usually need at least two turned up.
