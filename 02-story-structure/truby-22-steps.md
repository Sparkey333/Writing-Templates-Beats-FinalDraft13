# Truby's 22 Steps

> Structure grows organically from your hero's moral need — not from page counts.
> — **John Truby**, *The Anatomy of Story: 22 Steps to Becoming a Master Storyteller* (2007)

Truby rejects rigid beat‑by‑page models. His 22 steps are an **organic, character‑driven**
sequence built around the hero's **moral and psychological weakness → self‑revelation**. The
seven he calls the essential "key steps" are marked ★ — hit those at minimum.

## When to use it
- Character‑ and theme‑heavy work; literary fiction; morally complex drama.
- When you want the plot to *argue* something about how to live (the antagonist attacks the
  hero's specific weakness; the ending is a moral choice).

## The 22 steps
1. **Self‑Revelation, Need, and Desire** ★ — decide up front what the hero must *learn* and
   *want* by the end; design backward from this.
2. **Ghost and Story World** — the wound from the past (the "ghost") + the world expressing it.
3. **Weakness and Need** ★ — the psychological *and moral* flaw the hero must overcome.
4. **Inciting Event** ★ — pushes the hero to form a goal and act.
5. **Desire** ★ — the concrete goal (the plot's spine).
6. **Ally or Allies** — friend(s) the hero voices the plan to.
7. **Opponent and/or Mystery** ★ — the rival competing for the *same* goal, who attacks the weakness.
8. **Fake‑Ally Opponent** — seems a friend, secretly an opponent (or vice versa).
9. **First Revelation & Decision** — new info changes desire/motive.
10. **Plan** — the strategy to beat the opponent.
11. **Opponent's Plan & Main Counterattack** — the rival's counter‑strategy.
12. **Drive** — escalating actions to win; often includes immoral steps.
13. **Attack by Ally** — an ally challenges the hero's questionable means.
14. **Apparent Defeat** — the lowest point; the opponent seems to have won.
15. **Second Revelation & Decision** — a revelation reignites an obsessive push.
16. **Audience Revelation** — the audience learns a key secret (maybe before the hero).
17. **Third Revelation & Decision** — final revelation drives the decisive confrontation.
18. **Gate, Gauntlet, Visit to Death** — narrowing pressure; a brush with (symbolic) death.
19. **Battle** ★ — the final conflict that decides who wins the goal.
20. **Self‑Revelation** ★ — the hero's climactic insight about themselves (psychological + moral).
21. **Moral Decision** — the hero *proves* the revelation by a choice between two courses.
22. **New Equilibrium** — desire gone; the hero exists at a permanently higher (or lower) level.

## The Truby throughline
**Weakness (3) → Desire (5) → Opponent attacks weakness (7) → Drive & immoral means (12) →
Apparent Defeat (14) → Self‑Revelation (20) → Moral Decision (21).** If those seven connect,
the story has a moral argument.

## Blank template
```text
TITLE:
WEAKNESS (psychological / moral):
NEED (what they must learn):
DESIRE (concrete goal):
GHOST (past wound):
OPPONENT (attacks the weakness, wants same goal):
SELF-REVELATION (climactic insight):
MORAL DECISION (the proving choice):
→ then fill the remaining 15 connective steps.
```
