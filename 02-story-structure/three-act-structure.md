# Three‑Act Structure

> Beginning, middle, end — with a turn between each.
> Rooted in Aristotle's *Poetics*; codified for screen by **Syd Field**, *Screenplay* (1979).

The load‑bearing wall of Western storytelling. Everything else in this folder is, more or
less, a finer‑grained slicing of these three acts. Proportions: **Act 1 ≈ 25%, Act 2 ≈ 50%,
Act 3 ≈ 25%.**

## When to use it
- Always available as the container. Layer a finer framework
  ([Save the Cat](./save-the-cat-15-beats.md), the [Sequence Method](./sequence-method.md))
  inside it.
- The one thing to get right: **the two turning points** (~25% and ~75%). If those land,
  the story has shape.

## The map (pages assume a ~110‑page script)
| Element | Position | % | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACT 1 — Setup** | pp. 1–27 | 0–25% | Hero, world, tone, stakes. |
| Inciting Incident | ~p. 10–12 | ~10% | The event that disrupts the status quo. |
| **Plot Point 1** | ~p. 25–27 | ~25% | Reversal that spins us into Act 2 and locks the central conflict. |
| **ACT 2 — Confrontation** | pp. 27–83 | 25–75% | Rising action, obstacles, escalation (longest act). |
| Pinch Point 1 | ~p. 37 | ~37% | Reminder of the antagonist's power. |
| **Midpoint** | ~p. 55 | ~50% | Major reversal/revelation; point of no return. |
| Pinch Point 2 | ~p. 68 | ~62% | A stronger squeeze from the opposition. |
| **Plot Point 2** | ~p. 82–88 | ~75% | Dramatic reversal (often the "all is lost" low); launches Act 3. |
| **ACT 3 — Resolution** | pp. 83–110 | 75–100% | Build to and through the climax; resolve. |
| Climax | ~p. 95–110 | ~90% | Highest‑tension confrontation; the dramatic question is answered. |
| Resolution / Denouement | final pages | ~end | Loose ends tied; new equilibrium shown. |

> Field's original "Paradigm" was pegged to **120** pages (PP1 ≈ p.25–27, Midpoint ≈ p.60,
> PP2 ≈ p.85–90). Scale to your length; the **percentages** are what travel.

## The "Act 2 problem"
Most sagging scripts sag in Act 2. Fixes: (1) a strong **Midpoint** reversal that flips the
goal or raises stakes; (2) **pinch points** that keep the antagonist pressing; (3) treat
Act 2 as two halves — *reaction* (25–50%, hero on the back foot) then *action* (50–75%,
hero proactive). See the [Sequence Method](./sequence-method.md) for a finer Act‑2 grid.
