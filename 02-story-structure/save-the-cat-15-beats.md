# Save the Cat! — The 15‑Beat Sheet

> A story is about a flawed hero who, through the events of the plot, is forced to change.
> — Blake Snyder, *Save the Cat! The Last Book on Screenwriting You'll Ever Need* (2005)

The "Blake Snyder Beat Sheet" (BS2) is the most‑used commercial beat sheet in film. It's
calibrated to a **110‑page screenplay** (≈1 page = 1 minute), which makes it unusually
concrete: each beat has a target page.

## What it is
15 beats that take a flawed protagonist from an "before" state, through a transformative
journey, to an "after" state that proves change. The engine is the **theme**: the story
argues a thesis about how to live, the hero resists it, and the plot forces them to accept it.

## When to use it
- Commercial/genre film and TV, and any novel that wants film‑tight pacing.
- When you want unambiguous checkpoints ("am I at my Midpoint by the halfway mark?").
- Less ideal for meandering literary or plotless slice‑of‑life work — try
  [Kishōtenketsu](./kishotenketsu.md) or [Story Circle](./story-circle.md) there.

## The 15 beats

| # | Beat | Page (of 110) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening Image** | 1 | The "before" snapshot — tone, mood, hero's starting state. |
| 2 | **Theme Stated** | 5 | Someone (usually *not* the hero) states what the story is really about. |
| 3 | **Set‑Up** | 1–10 | Hero, world, flaws, and "things that need fixing." Introduce the A‑cast. |
| 4 | **Catalyst** | 12 | The inciting incident — life‑changing news/event that breaks the status quo. |
| 5 | **Debate** | 12–25 | The hero hesitates: *should I do this?* Last look at the old world. |
| 6 | **Break into Two** | 25 | Hero makes a **choice** and enters the new world (Act 2). |
| 7 | **B Story** | 30 | A secondary story/relationship (love interest, mentor) that carries the theme. |
| 8 | **Fun and Games** | 30–55 | The "promise of the premise" — the trailer moments; concept delivered. |
| 9 | **Midpoint** | 55 | A **false victory or false defeat**; stakes raise, story pivots. |
| 10 | **Bad Guys Close In** | 55–75 | External pressure mounts, internal doubts regroup; the team frays. |
| 11 | **All Is Lost** | 75 | The lowest point; often a "whiff of death" (something/someone dies). |
| 12 | **Dark Night of the Soul** | 75–85 | The hero wallows in hopelessness before finding the answer. |
| 13 | **Break into Three** | 85 | The solution appears (A + B stories fuse); hero re‑engages (Act 3). |
| 14 | **Finale** | 85–110 | Hero executes the plan, beats the bad guys, proves change; new world built. |
| 15 | **Final Image** | 110 | The "after" — mirror‑opposite of the Opening Image; shows transformation. |

> Snyder's sequel *Save the Cat! Goes to the Movies* adds a **"5‑Point Finale"**
> (Gathering the Team → Storming the Castle → High Tower Surprise → Dig Deep Down →
> Execution of the New Plan) to expand beat 14.

## Worked example — *Die Hard* (compressed)
- **Opening Image:** John McClane, white‑knuckled on a plane, an outsider flying to LA.
- **Theme Stated:** "You just have to decide to be somewhere and be there" — reconciliation.
- **Catalyst (~12):** Terrorists seize Nakatomi Plaza.
- **Break into Two (~25):** McClane slips away barefoot and commits to fighting back alone.
- **Midpoint (~55):** He kills a lead henchman, gets a radio, and the FBI/cops arrive —
  a false "help is coming" that makes things worse.
- **All Is Lost (~75):** Gruber captures Holly; the vault opens; the plan is working.
- **Finale:** Rooftop, the tape‑gun reveal, "Yippee‑ki‑yay," Gruber falls.
- **Final Image:** McClane reunited with Holly — the outsider now belongs.

 1. OPENING IMAGE (p.1) —

 3. SET-UP (p.1–10) — flaws/things to fix:

 5. DEBATE (p.12–25) — the question the hero asks:
 6. BREAK INTO TWO (p.25) — the choice:
 7. B STORY (p.30) — relationship that carries the theme:
 8. FUN AND GAMES (p.30–55) — promise of the premise:
 9. MIDPOINT (p.55) — false victory [ ] / false defeat [ ]:
