# The Fichtean Curve

> Start late, cut the throat‑clearing, and hit them with crisis after crisis.
> Named for J.G. Fichte's dialectic; popularized in fiction craft (e.g., John Gardner).

A structure that **minimizes exposition** and builds through a **series of escalating
crises** to the climax. Where the classic pyramid has a long, gentle rising action, the
Fichtean curve is a jagged staircase — every step is a mini‑crisis that raises the stakes.

## When to use it
- Thrillers, action, horror, mystery, page‑turners — anything that should feel relentless.
- When you want to open *in medias res* (in the middle of things) and backfill only as needed.

## The shape
```
                              CLIMAX ●
                          ● crisis
                    ● crisis
              ● crisis
        ● crisis
  ● INCITING (start here, already in motion)
  └─ rising action = a chain of crises, each higher than the last ─┘
      then a short falling action → resolution
```

## The parts
1. **Inciting Incident (early!)** — begin near the first crisis; establish world through
   action, not front‑loaded exposition.
2. **Rising Action = a series of crises** — each complication is a genuine mini‑climax with a
   setback; each ratchets tension and reveals character. Backstory is dripped in *between*
   crises, only when needed.
3. **Climax** — the biggest crisis; the central question is answered.
4. **Falling Action / Resolution** — kept short; get out fast.

## Contrast with Freytag
- [Freytag](./freytags-pyramid.md): long setup, symmetric rise and fall.
- Fichtean: tiny setup, **multiple peaks** on the way up, brief descent. Better for modern
  pacing and genre fiction.

## Worked example — a thriller opening
Don't open with the detective's biography. Open on the body. Crisis 1: the wrong suspect is
arrested. Crisis 2: a second victim. Crisis 3: the detective's own alibi implodes. Crisis 4:
the partner is taken. Climax: the trap and the reveal. Resolution: two pages, then out.

## Blank template
```text
TITLE:
OPEN ON (a moment already in motion, near crisis 1):
CRISIS 1 —  (setback:)
CRISIS 2 —  (setback:)
CRISIS 3 —  (setback:)
CRISIS 4 —  (setback:)
CLIMAX (biggest crisis; question answered) —
RESOLUTION (short) —
EXPOSITION plan: what backstory drips in AFTER which crisis?
```
