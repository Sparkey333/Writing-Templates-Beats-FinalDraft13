# Seven‑Point Story Structure

> Start at the end. Then figure out how to get there.
> — Dan Wells (novelist), popularized in his 5‑part YouTube lecture series; rooted in the
> *Star Trek RPG* narrative model.

Seven plot points you design **backward**: nail the Resolution first, then set a Hook that's
its mirror‑opposite, then fill the turns between.

## When to use it
- Novels and series, especially when you know your ending/theme but not the middle.
- Excellent for making the beginning and ending *rhyme* (the hero ends as the opposite of
  how they began).

## The 7 points
| # | Point | Position | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | start | The starting state — ideally the **opposite** of the Resolution. |
| 2 | **Plot Turn 1** | ~1/4 | The call to adventure; the world changes, the story problem enters. |
| 3 | **Pinch Point 1** | ~3/8 | Apply pressure; introduce/reveal the antagonist's force. Something goes wrong. |
| 4 | **Midpoint** | ~1/2 | The shift from **reaction to action** — hero stops fleeing and starts pursuing. |
| 5 | **Pinch Point 2** | ~5/8 | Harder pressure; the jaws close; often a mentor/ally lost. |
| 6 | **Plot Turn 2** | ~3/4 | The hero gets the final thing/knowledge needed to win (the last piece). |
| 7 | **Resolution** | end | The climax and payoff — the state you designed first. |

## The backward method
1. Write the **Resolution** (7): where/who is the hero at the end?
2. Write the **Hook** (1) as its opposite.
3. Write the **Midpoint** (4): the moment they turn from passive to active.
4. Fill **Plot Turns** (2, 6): the two big direction changes.
5. Fill **Pinch Points** (3, 5): the two escalations of pressure.

## Worked example — *The Hunger Games* (compressed)
Hook: Katniss, powerless, surviving in District 12. PT1: the reaping / she volunteers.
Pinch 1: the Games begin, Careers hunt her. Midpoint: the rule change (two can win) —
she shifts from hiding to allying/acting. Pinch 2: Rue's death; the stakes turn personal
and political. PT2: the final rule reversal; she reaches for the berries. Resolution:
survives, now a symbol of rebellion — the opposite of the powerless girl at the Hook.

## Blank template
```text
TITLE:                THEME:
7. RESOLUTION (write FIRST) —
1. HOOK (opposite of #7) —
4. MIDPOINT (reaction → action) —
2. PLOT TURN 1 (~1/4) —
6. PLOT TURN 2 (~3/4, final piece) —
3. PINCH POINT 1 (~3/8) —
5. PINCH POINT 2 (~5/8) —

For a multi-POV novel, run one 7-point line per POV/subplot, then interleave.
```
